Privacy Policy

Effective: May 19, 2026 · Last updated: May 19, 2026

The short version

PortPilot is a local-only macOS utility. It collects no personal data, sends no telemetry, contacts no remote servers, and shares nothing with third parties. Everything happens on your Mac.

1. Who this policy is from

This Privacy Policy explains how PortPilot ("the app", "we", "us") handles information when you install and use the application on macOS. By using PortPilot, you agree to the practices described here.

2. Data we collect

We collect no data. PortPilot has no backend server, no cloud sync, no external database, no analytics SDK, no crash reporter, and no AI service. Nothing you do in the app is transmitted to us or to any third party.

To do its job, PortPilot reads local system state on your Mac using standard tools:

  • lsof — to list which processes are listening on which TCP ports.
  • ps — to read the command line, working directory, and metadata of those processes.
  • docker (if installed) — to list running Docker containers and their port mappings.

The output of these tools is processed entirely in memory inside the app and shown to you. It is never written to a log, never copied to disk, and never sent over the network.

3. Data stored on your device

PortPilot stores a small amount of preference data locally on your Mac so the app remembers how you like it set up. This data lives only on your machine. We have no way to read it.

  • Theme preference — System, Light, or Dark.
  • Search query — the last text you typed into the search box.
  • Filter preference — whether protected system processes are shown.
  • Custom aliases — friendly names you've assigned to ports.

These preferences are kept in your browser-style local storage and the app's sandboxed Application Support directory. They are not synced with iCloud or any other service.

4. Network activity

PortPilot makes no outbound network requests. It does not check for updates over the network (the Mac App Store handles updates), does not report errors home, and does not contact any analytics or advertising service.

When you click the Open action on a port, PortPilot asks macOS to open the corresponding http://localhost:<port> URL in your default browser. That request goes only to your own local machine.

5. Third-party sharing

PortPilot integrates no third-party SDKs, no advertising networks, no analytics partners, and no tracking technologies. Because we collect no data, we have no data to share.

6. Data retention and deletion

The only data PortPilot retains are the local preferences listed in Section 3. You can delete all of them at any time by:

  • Opening the app's Settings panel and resetting individual preferences, or
  • Uninstalling PortPilot from your Mac, which removes all stored preferences along with the app.

Because we never receive a copy of your data, there is no remote record to delete. Uninstalling is the complete and final deletion.

7. Children's privacy

PortPilot is a developer utility and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including individuals under the age of 13.

8. Security

PortPilot ships through the Mac App Store with Apple's sandbox enabled. It uses the minimum permissions needed to read local process information and to stop processes the user explicitly selects. Stop actions send SIGTERM first; SIGKILL requires a second, explicit confirmation. Protected system processes cannot be stopped.

Sandboxed by Apple

The App Store build of PortPilot runs in Apple's App Sandbox. It can only see the processes and files macOS explicitly grants it access to. There is no escalated privilege, no helper daemon, and no kernel extension.

9. Your rights

Because we do not collect, process, or store any personal data on our servers, the typical access / correction / portability rights under GDPR, CCPA, and similar frameworks have no remote data to apply to. All of your data lives on your Mac and is fully under your control.

10. Changes to this policy

If we ever change how PortPilot handles data, we will update this page and revise the "Last updated" date at the top. A material change (for example, if PortPilot ever started collecting data) would also be announced in the app and in the App Store release notes before it took effect.
